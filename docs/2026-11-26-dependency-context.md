# Question-rooted context experiment, 26 November 2026

## Design

Each arm opens an isolated compiler session, one Lain model worker, two lexical frames and one local Datomic catalog. The same worker receives each recognition and answer request. Recognition gets the question and archive metadata. It emits IDs. A session-side Clojure experiment driver reads their transitive parents in journal order, dispatches any selected fixture tools, routes receipts by the persisted call edges, and constructs the next packet. Full history uses the same recognition step and tool interface but includes every visible event body in the answer packet. A selected ID is Lain’s proposed evidence. The network independently inspects TTMS support for the executable price and arithmetic cases. The driver owns scheduling in this prototype. [Lain network](../experiments/dependency-context/runtime.lain) · [Experiment driver and scorer](../experiments/dependency-context/probe.clj)

The price and bracket questions reuse the historical wording, malformed function, executable checks and candidate price networks. Arithmetic reuses the three original questions, with the first operand and subsequent results exposed as live bindings. Each arm has six scored roots: quote, repair, three arithmetic questions, and a new historical-versus-revised quote. A separate mutation check observes the price after changing the order from 140 to 141 and re-reads the unrelated arithmetic result. Evidence has a stable ID within the arm, UTC acceptance time, monotonic sequence and parent references. Time in the follow-up is represented by explicit revision and observation edges. This tests before/after reasoning on an ordered change.

The overlap schedule opens price, repair and arithmetic together. Tool completions are released in reverse call order. A diagnostic for draft revision 2 is also offered to each outstanding revision-1 reader call. The handler compares the observed revision-specific task with the call’s task before waking its root. Later arithmetic follow-ups have an explicit parent and an intervening independent recorded arithmetic answer. The reader uses the existing native parser on the archived draft. Tool delivery order is controlled by the fixture, so these timings exclude real external job latency.

## Preliminary evidence

Scripted checks initially exposed missing lexical environment facilities in the prototype. After registering the existing Soul extension, the controls passed. In the first live pilot, both full-history arms passed all six roots. Both selected-context arms passed bracket repair and the arithmetic chain. Both found the identifying invoice and chose the correct pricing rule, but one omitted the QUOTE tag and one used the archive lookup ID instead of the printed invoice ID. The dependent historical-price check consequently failed in both. The pilot wrote all four summaries; its process later exited with a source-reader error after its temporary source copy was replaced during execution. The final trial below uses its own untouched snapshot, a more explicit price action description, a bounded correction and a wrong-revision receipt challenge. Pilot results are retained separately. [Pilot artifacts](/home/lain/reports/dependency-context-live-20261126)

In the next matched trial the generic quote rejection still left three of four sessions choosing the whole-order rule after correction. Serial full and selected both passed 4/6, overlapping full passed 5/6 and overlapping selected passed 4/6. Bracket and arithmetic passed throughout. All four scripted controls passed. A follow-up kept the same call cap and recorded the actual TTMS callable predictions at the invoice quantity in the correction receipt. Only an obtained invoice belonging to the current question can unlock that receipt. The provider must still select and submit the callable. Scripted controls deliberately submit a wrong first quote to exercise this path. ## Final measured comparison with callable feedback

| Schedule | Answer context | Supported roots | Calls | Answer-stage input tokens including correction | Total tokens across both stages | Summed model-call seconds |
| --- | --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Serial | Full history | 5/6 | 13 | 11,953 | 16,110 | 13.02 |
| Serial | Selected dependencies | 5/6 | 13 | 5,030 | 9,187 | 10.76 |
| Overlapping | Full history | 6/6 | 13 | 15,506 | 19,903 | 13.16 |
| Overlapping | Selected dependencies | 4/6 | 13 | 4,225 | 8,622 | 9.86 |

All four live arms first submitted the wrong price rule, then used the concrete receipt and submitted the marginal rule. The network returned 26,000 cents with the invoice, policy and target-count premises. Every repaired function was admitted and returned 15,800, 20,000, 21,050 and 26,450 on the held-out inputs. Arithmetic returned −2980, −149 and 1 with compiled references to previous results and TTMS support from the initial operand. The quantity revision updated the marginal quote to 26,150 while the arithmetic result stayed at 1. Both overlapping schedules rejected the wrong draft diagnostic; their valid repair receipts returned to repair and the price receipt returned to price.

The strict score also requires the selected packet to contain every required evidence ID (the full-history packet naturally contains more IDs), and the relevant tool to have been requested by the same root. In both serial arms the model fixed the bracket without requesting the reader diagnostic, so repair lost its provenance point. In overlapping selected context the price recognizer chose policy and invoice but omitted the target-quantity record. The executable still had target-count support, and the exact question included 140, yet the selected packet lacked that archive ID. The historical follow-up returned both correct amounts as JSON strings. The verifier required integer amounts and a supported original price answer. Overlapping full history passed both checks.

Across the two schedules, selection used 43% and 57% fewer total tokens, respectively. Every arm made 13 real calls. The latency figures depend on the local worker and workload. Selectors also picked incidental records on some turns. Neither smaller packets nor executable correctness guarantees complete or minimal evidence. There were four isolated scripted controls with all 24 scored roots and four mutation checks passing. Local artifacts include every recognition, answer and correction packet, responses, answer-parent lists, timestamps, tool receipts, and Datomic databases. Compact results in the repository include question-level latencies.
